# One Recorded Call Now Covers Compliant Phone Enrollment

As of October 10, 2026, a solo independent broker can take the Scope of Appointment, review needs, present a plan, take the application, and capture the enrollment attestation on a **single recorded phone call**, because the CY2027 final rule (CMS-4208-F3, 91 FR 17384) deleted the 48-hour SOA waiting period and CMS's May 2026 HPMS memo confirms the new SOA rules apply to CY2027 marketing beginning October 1, 2026 ([CMS HPMS SOA FAQs](https://webdocs.hallrender.com/wp-content/uploads/HPMS-Scope-of-Appointment-FAQs.pdf); [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2026/06/01/cms-revises-medicare-advantage-marketing-guidance-for-scope-of-appointment-forms/)). The CMS-mandated core is small: record the whole call from before the consent line through the confirmation number; capture a verbal SOA with five elements (products, date, your identity, the client's identity, and the no-obligation/no-effect/no-auto-enroll statement); read the TPMO disclaimer before any benefit is mentioned; review the Pre-Enrollment Checklist topics and say where the Summary of Benefits and Star Ratings live; take the application; and obtain a clear verbal attestation that the client knows they are enrolling. Nearly everything else that appears in FMO scripts (the "I may be compensated" line, listing every product category, an explicit "do you consent to recording" question outside all-party-consent states, text-then-voice SOA preferences) is a carrier or FMO add-on. The real gatekeeper for a solo broker is not CMS but the carrier's approved telephonic mechanism: UHC, Wellcare/Aetna (Ascend RATE), Devoted and Independent Health let you capture the voice signature on your own recorded line inside their e-app, while Humana, Anthem, SCAN and Clover route the signature through a carrier recorded line that issues a code. Keep the recording **10 years** (the enrollment portion remains an enrollment record even though marketing-call retention dropped to 6 years), and expect kick-backs mainly from missing MBI/address/Part A-B data, election-period mismatches, missing SOA coverage, and recording gaps. A caveat that applies to this whole report: every underlying page was reachable only through search-result snippets, so verbatim regulatory wording and carrier script text should be verified against the linked PDFs before you adopt them.

## The 48-hour wait is gone, so SOA, presentation and enrollment fit one call

The single biggest simplification for a phone-first broker arrived this month. The CY2027 final rule, published April 6, 2026, revised 42 CFR 422.2264(c)(3) and 423.2264(c)(3) to remove the requirement that the SOA be agreed "at least 48 hours prior to the personal marketing appointment," and redefined a personal marketing appointment as one that "may take place in person, telephonically or virtually" ([Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2026/06/01/cms-revises-medicare-advantage-marketing-guidance-for-scope-of-appointment-forms/)). The rule's general effective date was June 1, 2026, but CMS's own HPMS "Scope of Appointment FAQs" memo (dated May 25 or 26, 2026, depending on the source) states the new rules "apply to CY 2027 marketing beginning October 1, 2026," and FMOs told agents to keep honoring the 48-hour wait through September 30 ([CMS HPMS SOA FAQs](https://webdocs.hallrender.com/wp-content/uploads/HPMS-Scope-of-Appointment-FAQs.pdf); [YourFMO FAQ](https://yourfmo.com/frequently-asked-questions-related-to-new-scope-of-appointment-rules/)). Since today is October 10, the June-versus-October debate is moot: **the SOA can be taken at the top of the same call** and the agent may proceed immediately, as long as the SOA is completed before any specific MA or Part D plan is discussed ([Smart Agent CRM](https://smartagentcrm.com/resources/medicare-scope-of-appointment)). The same rule package removed the 12-hour educational-to-marketing gap ([RISE Health](https://www.risehealth.org/insights-articles/article/7-marketing-changes-in-the-2027-medicare-advantage-final-rule/)).

Two things did not change. The SOA is still required for every personal marketing appointment, including inbound calls; Humana's guardrails say an SOA "must be completed for all personal marketing appointments" and a verbal SOA on an inbound call "must include all required details of a valid SOA and be recorded" ([Humana guardrails via YourFMO](https://yourfmo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/8ef8e131-1e76-4108-8cfc-df5b473d7d03-TICKET.supporting_files-Humana-External-Marketing-Guardrails-July-2025-1.pdf)). And the SOA remains product-specific and valid for 12 months: the CMS memo says agents "may contact beneficiaries regarding the agreed upon scope of products documented in the SOA" for 12 months from signature, and a different product type needs a new SOA ([CMS HPMS SOA FAQs](https://webdocs.hallrender.com/wp-content/uploads/HPMS-Scope-of-Appointment-FAQs.pdf)). Critically for a phone broker, the memo also says "an audio or audio-visual recording or an electronic record would suffice as an SOA record for a personal marketing appointment that does not occur in person," so **no signed form is needed for a phone appointment**; the recording is the SOA ([CMS HPMS SOA FAQs](https://webdocs.hallrender.com/wp-content/uploads/HPMS-Scope-of-Appointment-FAQs.pdf)). One vendor (EnrollHere) still claims the no-obligation language "must appear on the SOA form itself" and is not sufficient verbally; that conflicts with the CMS memo and should be disregarded for telephonic appointments ([EnrollHere Academy](https://academy.enrollhere.com/Scope-of-Appointment-SOA-Compliance-1afbe273c76380b58a58d5d0107b1825)). Expect carrier lag, though: Molina's 2026 Enrollment Handbook, written before the change, still recites the 48-hour rule ([Molina 2026 Handbook](https://molinaagentcenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Medicare-Enrollment-Handbook-2026-FINAL.pdf)), and carriers may keep stricter internal rules ([Quotedeck](https://quotedeck.app/guides/scope-of-appointment-rules-2027)).

The TPMO disclaimer timing also loosened. For CY2027 marketing, CMS "required plans to verbally convey the disclaimer before benefits are discussed, rather than within the first minute of a call" and "removed 'State Health Insurance Assistance Programs (SHIPs)' from the disclaimer" ([CMS CY2027 marketing guideline changes](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/changes-medicare-advantage-organization-medicare-drug-plan-sponsors-marketing-guidelines.pdf)). The post-October 1 wording, per secondary sources, is: "We do not offer every plan available in your area. Currently we represent [X] organizations which offer [Y] products in your area. Please contact Medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE to get information on all of your options." ([TMS Brokerage](https://tmsbrokerage.com/2026/09/18/the-cy2027-medicare-marketing-rules-go-live-october-1-heres-the-final-prep-playbook-for-independent-agents/); [New Horizons](https://blog.newhorizonsmktg.com/tpmo-disclaimer-tips-to-stay-compliant)). Sources conflict on the middle sentence; the alternate CMS-permitted version is "Any information we provide is limited to those plans we do offer in your area" for TPMOs that cannot count plans ([Action Benefits](https://blog.actionbenefits.com/stay-compliant-with-medicares-tpmo-disclaimer)). Older checklists such as Warner Pacific's still say "within the first 60 seconds" ([Warner Pacific checklist](https://login.warnerpacific.com/getmedia/7f54fcd2-0365-408b-a279-5f82a168c8e5/Broker-Checklist-Phone-and-Enrollments.pdf)); reading it early is still the simplest habit because it satisfies both the old and new timing, and the exact amended CFR text of §422.2267(e)(41) could not be read in this research, so verify the wording with your FMO.

## The CMS-mandated core is nine steps; everything else is an add-on

Carrier and CMS sources converge on the same skeleton for a sales-to-enrollment call. The Humana-provided PY2026 Multiplan Telephonic Enrollment Script opens with "Please know our call will be recorded for quality and training purposes; is it ok if I continue?" and instructs the agent to end the call if the beneficiary objects ([PY2026 Multiplan Enrollment Script](https://cornerstoneseniormarketing.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/MULTIPLAN_IMG_ENRLSCRIPT26_M-Humana-Enrollment-Script.pdf)); CMS's 2022 FAQ is blunter: "If a beneficiary declines to have their call with a TPMO recorded, the sales agent must end the call" ([CMS Agent/Broker FAQs 10-19-2022](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/agent-broker-marketing-faqs-10-19-2022.pdf)). The SOA element list that Aetna and BCBS attribute to CMS is: "product types to be discussed, date of appointment, as well as beneficiary and agent contact information," plus a statement "that there is no obligation to enroll, current or future Medicare enrollment status will not be impacted, and automatic enrollment will not occur" ([Aetna via PFS](https://pfsinsurance.com/news/medicare/aetna-091423); [BCBSIL](https://www.bcbsilcommunications.com/newsletters/nftb/2023/1011/stories/NLT_PROD_NFTB_IL_101123_SCOPE_OF_APPOINTMENT.html)). CMS itself "does not provide a sample SOA form" ([Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2026/06/01/cms-revises-medicare-advantage-marketing-guidance-for-scope-of-appointment-forms/)), which is why forum agents report that "there is no script" and that they "built their own script from those CMS elements" ([insurance-forums thread 111587](https://www.insurance-forums.com/community/threads/is-it-possible-to-do-a-verbal-soa.111587)).

On the enrollment side, 42 CFR 422.2267(e)(4) requires that "for telephonic enrollments, the contents of the PECL must be reviewed with the prospective enrollee prior to the completion of the enrollment," a requirement added by the CY2024 rule ([42 CFR 422.2267](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.2267); [CY2024 final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/04/12/2023-07115/medicare-program-contract-year-2024-policy-and-technical-changes-to-the-medicare-advantage-program)), and 423.2267(e)(17) says "for telephonic enrollment, the beneficiary must be verbally told where they can access the Star Ratings Document," with CMS enrollment guidance adding the Summary of Benefits ([42 CFR 423.2267](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/423.2267); [CMS MA Enrollment Guidance](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy-2024-ma-enrollment-and-disenrollment-guidance.pdf)). The PECL's eight topics are eligibility, providers, drug costs, health needs and costs, premiums, plan benefits, effect of a coverage change, and administrative items; a verbal waiver by the client does not excuse the review ([Open Exam Prep AHIP guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ahip-medicare/enrollment-disclosure-requirements/pre-enrollment-checklist), secondary). For the attestation itself, CMS guidance says a telephonic enrollment "must have followed the approved script, included a clear statement that the individual understands they are requesting enrollment, and have been recorded," and the application date is the date of the call ([CMS MA Enrollment Guidance](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy-2024-ma-enrollment-and-disenrollment-guidance.pdf)); the founding 2005 memo adds "a statement of the individual's agreement to be recorded, all required elements necessary to complete the enrollment, and a verbal attestation of the intent to enroll" ([CMS 10/5/2005 memo](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/health-plans/healthplansgeninfo/downloads/ch2memomaosfinal10-05-05.pdf)). Humana's internal script guide adds a point that compliance reviewers actually check: the enrollee says "yes" and states name and date of birth, and agents "are NOT permitted to only tell them to say 'yes' without giving them an option to say 'no'" ([Humana DMS-044](https://humana.kmsihosting.com/dotnet/application/downloadcontent2.aspx?content_id=28498)). When you enroll through a carrier e-app (JarvisEnroll, Ascend RATE, Enrollment Hub), the carrier-approved attestation text is embedded, so you satisfy the "approved script" element without filing anything yourself.

## Carriers split into "your line" and "their line" signature models

CMS does not care which recording tool you use ("CMS cannot recommend a particular brand or mechanism" ([CMS FAQs 2022](https://CMS.gov/files/document/agent-broker-marketing-faqs-10-19-2022.pdf))), but each carrier does, and forum veterans say the practical first question is always "which carrier's approved telephonic enrollment process are you using?" ([insurance-forums thread 108941](https://www.insurance-forums.com/community/threads/medicare-advantage-marketing-compliance.108941/page-4)). The sources sort carriers into three models. **Model A, agent-recorded voice signature inside the e-app**, is the simplest for a solo broker: UHC's JarvisEnroll (the successor to LEAN) offers "Voice Signature ... to allow a remote experience for those without an email address," usable "if you're on a recorded call with your client" ([UHC Jarvis](https://www.uhcjarvis.com/content/jarvis/en/secure/knowledge_center/Enrollment.html); [HealthSherpa](https://intercom.help/healthsherpa-medicare-help-center/en/articles/16114065-united-enrollment)); Wellcare and Aetna use Ascend's RATE (Remote Agent Telephonic Enrollment), which "allows you to conduct a telephonic enrollment while recording the phone call with the beneficiary's permission," and will not proceed if the client declines recording ([Wellcare broker page](https://www.wellcare.com/en/broker-resources/application-and-enrollment); [TBR Wellcare guide](https://www.tbrins.com/uploads/9/5/9/7/95973204/wellcare_enrollment_guide.pdf)); Devoted lets an agent on a recorded line read the Enrollee Signature content and take the verbal signature, with "the agent's call recording [as] proof of signature" ([Devoted Quick Start](https://www.empowerbrokerage.com/downloads/callcenter/Enrollment-Scripts/Devoted-Health-Instructions.pdf)); and Independent Health's script states "this call is being recorded and that will serve as your electronic signature" ([Independent Health 2026 script](https://www.independenthealth.com/content/dam/independenthealth/broker/broker-partner-resources/documents/2026-telephonic-enrollment-script.pdf)).

**Model B, carrier line issues a code**, adds a three-way step. Humana's IVR telephonic signature "was built for Field Agents" and requires "the ability to perform a three-way call" with the recorded IVR, with Spanish via a DMS-assisted route ([Gainsystem Humana IVR](https://www.gainsystem.com/agentsite/tools/Remote%20Enrollment/Humana.pdf)); Humana's Enrollment Hub also offers "Telephonic Signature" with language preference ([AIM](https://whyaim.com/humana-enrollment-hub-telephonic-signature-and-language-preference/)). Anthem's Voice Signature Recording Tool has the agent dial a recording line with an access code, choose English or Spanish and MA or Part D, have the applicant answer at least ten prompts and state name and date, then key the resulting call ID into the e-app; "all enrollments must be done on an INBOUND CALL" ([MedicareHelper Anthem tool](https://medicarehelper.net/anthemVoiceSignatureRecordingTool), undated third-party). SCAN's "Telephonic Confirmation of Prospect Intent to Enroll" connects the prospect to a SCAN representative by three-way call, who confirms SOA and intent and gives a confirmation code for the signature line ([SCAN process PDF](https://agentportal.scanhealthplan.com/-/media/agent-portal/documents/telephonic-confirmation-of-prospect-intent-to-enroll-process.pdf)); Clover's TAE line works the same way ([Clover TAE QRG](https://cdn.cloverhealth.com/filer_public/cd/ac/cdacb0fb-63d7-485a-8240-df427d677e07/telephonic_assisted_enrollment_qrg_ch_010_092021.pdf)). **Model C, carrier callback**, is Devoted's unrecorded-line path: the agent reads the signature prompt, and Devoted calls the client within two business days to take the verbal signature ([Devoted Quick Start](https://www.empowerbrokerage.com/downloads/callcenter/Enrollment-Scripts/Devoted-Health-Instructions.pdf)).

Two caveats matter. First, the most uncertain point in the research is whether Humana and UHC currently let independent field agents (as opposed to authorized call centers) complete phone enrollments; the restrictive statements ("only UnitedHealthcare-approved and authorized call centers are permitted to conduct telephonic enrollments," "Humana only allows captives to sell by phone") are all pre-2022 forum posts, while the existence of 2025-2026 JarvisEnroll Voice Signature and Humana Enrollment Hub telephonic-signature guides implies the restriction was relaxed, but no 2026 primary document confirmed this ([insurance-forums thread 84315](https://insurance-forums.com/community/threads/no-more-lean-for-uhc-field-agents.84315/); [UHC Jarvis Sales Tool Guides](https://www.uhcjarvis.com/content/jarvis/en/secure/knowledge_center/referenceGuides/salesToolsGuides.html)). Confirm with each carrier's broker support line (UHC Producer Help Desk 1-888-381-8581) before AEP. Second, Anthem/Elevance pulled most standard MA plans from online broker platforms in May 2025 and reportedly made telesales non-commissionable, so phone-enrolling Anthem may not be worth the extra step ([Becker's](https://www.beckerspayer.com/payer/elevance-health-pulls-medicare-advantage-from-marketing-platforms/)). No Alignment Health telephonic process document surfaced at all. The practical lesson from practitioners is to standardize on one or two Model A carriers you know cold, since those flows collapse application and signature into a single call on your own recorded line.

## Record everything, keep it ten years, and watch the retention trap

The recording duty is the one rule with no exceptions. CMS's Agent/Broker Training guidelines require TPMOs to "record all marketing, sales, and enrollment calls, including the audio portion of calls via web-based technology, in their entirety" ([CMS CY2027 Training & Testing Guidelines](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy2027-agent-broker-training-testing-guidelines-pdf.pdf)); the 2022 FAQ applies this to independent agents, inbound and outbound, year-round, with in-person meetings exempt ([CMS FAQs 2022](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/agent-broker-marketing-faqs-10-19-2022.pdf)). The CY2024 rule narrowed the scope so that service calls (ID cards, claims help) need not be recorded ([PSM](https://www.psmbrokerage.com/blog/psm-guide-understanding-the-new-cms-call-recording-changes)). Start the recorder before you say the consent line, so the consent itself is captured, and never pause through holds, transfers or three-way calls ([NIMBroker](https://nimbroker.com/2025/07/09/call-recording-medicare/); [PSM](https://www.psmbrokerage.com/blog/call-recording-and-retention-requirements-for-telephonic-sales-of-medicare-plans)). A verbal SOA recording does not substitute for recording the full sales call ([PSM Voice SOA](https://www.psmbrokerage.com/blog/voice-soa-scope-of-appointment)).

Retention is where sources conflict most, and the conservative answer is simple. The CY2027 rule amended 422.2274(g)(2)(ii) to shorten marketing and sales call retention "from 10 years to 6 years," with audio required for the first three years and audio or a complete transcript for years four through six ([42 CFR 422.2274](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.2274); [Applied Policy](https://www.appliedpolicy.com/cms-finalizes-cy-2027-changes-to-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-to-implement-part-d-redesign-update-star-ratings-and-reduce-administrative-burden/)). But the enrollment portion "can serve as the enrollment form and proof that the beneficiary attested to an intent to enroll" and so remains an enrollment record on the **10-year** track ([Informed Plus Choice](https://informedpluschoice.com/insurance-agents/medicare-call-recording-requirements/); [Tech Savvy Insurance](https://techsavvyinsurance.com/blog/medicare-call-recording-retention-2027/)). The Medicare Rights Center summarized the change as "3 years, down from 10," which appears to misread the audio-only portion ([Medicare Rights Center](https://www.medicarerights.org/medicare-watch/2026/04/16/final-2027-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-rule-increases-plan-pay-and-relaxes-marketing-restrictions)), and Klariqo argues for eleven years counted from the end of the contract year ([Klariqo](https://klariqo.com/blog/medicare-tpmo-call-recording-rules-2026/), unverified). Because a single call contains both the sales portion and the enrollment, splitting retention saves nothing: **keep every sales-to-enrollment recording 10 years**, in WAV or MP3, named with client and date, in HIPAA-compliant storage you can produce on request ([PSM](https://www.psmbrokerage.com/blog/call-recording-and-retention-requirements-for-telephonic-sales-of-medicare-plans)). A second unresolved conflict: Senior Benefit Services and Ritter read the 2027 rule as "only calls that result in an enrollment must be recorded," while Informed Plus Choice and Carecycle say the duty to record all marketing and sales calls is unchanged and only retention shortened ([Senior Benefit Services](https://srbenefit.com/the-cms-2027-final-rule-key-updates-every-medicare-agent-should-understand/); [Informed Plus Choice](https://informedpluschoice.com/blog/marketing-call-vs-enrollment-recording-medicare-agents/)). Until the rule text is confirmed, keep recording every sales call; the downside of over-recording is storage, the downside of under-recording is a voided enrollment.

The retention trap practitioners warn about is mechanical, not legal: one agent's phone system purged recordings after 90 days, forcing manual downloads ([insurance-forums thread 108899](https://www.insurance-forums.com/community/threads/call-recording-ridiculousness.108899/page-8)). Make "download and file the recording" the last step of every enrollment call. If a required call fails to record, notify the carrier promptly (one vendor says within 48 hours) and document the failure ([Thunai](https://www.thunai.ai/blog/cms-call-recording-requirements)). CMS reviews recordings on complaint and at random, and expects the entity holding the recording to produce it ([NABIP](https://www.nabip.org/membership-resources/medicare-portal/medicare-marketing-rule-resources/cms-provides-some-clarification-on-how-to-comply-with-medicare-marketing-final-rule)); CMS's 2022 review found agents gave missing or inaccurate information on more than 80% of calls reviewed, which is what triggered the current regime ([AIS Health](https://aishealth.mmitnetwork.com/blogs/radar-on-medicare-advantage/cms-spooks-ma-plans-with-warning-about-secret-shopping-enhanced-monitoring)).

## Kick-backs come from data gaps and election codes, not from short scripts

The enrollments that get pended or voided fail on a short, predictable list. Data completeness leads: a valid request needs name, permanent residence address (a P.O. box alone is not acceptable), DOB, sex, phone, MBI, Part A/B status, plan selection, premium payment option, ESRD status and a dated attestation; an incomplete request pends while the plan chases missing data, which "is a delay on your clients' coverage and your commission" ([Open Exam Prep enrollment procedures](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ahip-medicare/enrollment-disclosure-requirements/enrollment-procedures-application-requirements); [Ritter 10 Tips](https://ritterim.com/blog/10-tips-to-submitting-an-error-free-medicare-enrollment-application/)). Election-period defects are second: "the selected period in the Attestation of Eligibility section needs to align with the selection of IEP, AEP, or SEP in the Agent/Office Use section," SEPs that require a date (loss of coverage) or extra information (Medicaid, Extra Help) must carry it, and "the wrong SEP code can get the application kicked back" ([Essential Care Agents](https://essentialcareagents.com/blog/10-tips-for-submitting-a-flawless-medicare-enrollment-application/); [GMIA](https://gmiainc.com/medicares-sep-are-you-using-the-correct-codes/)). These are carrier-side pends that cost time. The compliance layer is more dangerous because it can void the enrollment or generate a CTM complaint: no SOA or "scope creep" into a product not on the SOA (cross-selling dental or life mid-call is a classic flag) ([AgentTech](https://agenttech.io/blog/cms-compliant-enrollment-calls)), a missing or late TPMO disclaimer, a partial or unretrievable recording, and using an unauthorized channel or skipping the carrier's voice-signature code ([Clover TAE QRG](https://cdn.cloverhealth.com/filer_public/cd/ac/cdacb0fb-63d7-485a-8240-df427d677e07/telephonic_assisted_enrollment_qrg_ch_010_092021.pdf)). A practitioner podcast names SOA, full call recording and the TPMO disclaimer as the three rules generating the most agent violations ([Insurance Producers Guild](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2603798/episodes/19522040-ep19-2027-medicare-rules-faster-sales-more-liability)). A Humana secret-shopper summary adds the benefit items agents most often skipped: explaining the Part B premium and late enrollment penalties ([Agility](https://blog.enrollinsurance.com/2025/01/secret-shopper-results-all-medicare-agents-need-for-cms-compliant-selling/)), and a forum case of a carrier sending an LEP letter after an attestation it had "no record of" shows why the creditable-coverage attestation belongs on the recording ([insurance-forums thread 114900](https://www.insurance-forums.com/community/threads/late-enrollment-penalty-issue.114900)).

Practitioner habits that keep the call short all front-load work: have MBI, Part A/B dates, address, DOB, phone, Medicaid/LIS status, PCP and drug list in your CRM or pre-loaded in the e-app before dialing, because "navigating away from an online application increases the risk of losing your spot" ([Ritter 10 Tips](https://ritterim.com/blog/10-tips-to-submitting-an-error-free-medicare-enrollment-application/)); for outbound calls hold documented permission to contact (BRC, web form, or prior recorded request) ([New Horizons](https://blog.newhorizonsmktg.com/medicare-advantage-appointments-101-how-to-stay-compliant2984)); read the data back before the signature; and save the recording immediately. No source quantified a target call length; the advice is about sequence, not minutes.

On Spanish-speaking clients, the research found a clear principle and a documentation gap. CMS rules contain no language carve-out, so a Spanish call needs the identical elements ([CMS FAQs 2022](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/agent-broker-marketing-faqs-10-19-2022.pdf)). Carriers supply the compliant Spanish wording: Molina's Spanish telephonic guide has the agent "avisar al inicio que la llamada se graba" and state "no hay obligación de inscribirse en los planes, y la llamada no cambia la inscripción actual o futura ni inscribe automáticamente en un plan," then confirm understanding, name and phone ([Molina Spanish inbound guide](https://molinaagentcenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/2023-Medicare-Telephonic-Enrollment-Call-Guide-Inbound-SPANISH.pdf), 2023, mixed Spanish/English); Humana's IVR and Anthem's recording tool offer Spanish branches ([Gainsystem Humana IVR](https://www.gainsystem.com/agentsite/tools/Remote%20Enrollment/Humana.pdf); [MedicareHelper Anthem tool](https://medicarehelper.net/anthemVoiceSignatureRecordingTool)); and Spanish SOA forms exist from Imperial, Baylor Scott & White, MetroPlus and HealthSun that can serve as wording models ([BSW SOA SP](https://www.bswhealthplan.com/SiteCollectionDocuments/Individual/Medicare/Y0058_Scope-of-Appointment-SP.pdf)). Use the carrier's approved Spanish TPMO disclaimer rather than your own translation, since the disclaimer "must be used verbatim" ([PSM](https://www.psmbrokerage.com/blog/medicare-marketing-changes-in-2024-new-tpmo-disclaimer)). If an interpreter joins, the three-way call stays on the recording end to end and the beneficiary, not the interpreter or a family member, gives every attestation ([PSM](https://www.psmbrokerage.com/blog/call-recording-and-retention-requirements-for-telephonic-sales-of-medicare-plans)). Not found anywhere: whether carriers require English transcripts of Spanish recordings, whether family members may interpret, any FMO Spanish verbal SOA script, or any 2026 Spanish telephonic guide (the newest is Molina's 2023). Check each carrier portal for its current Spanish script before relying on the sample below.

## Recommended minimum script and checklist

The scripts below are synthesized from the CMS element lists and the shortest carrier wording found; they are **not CMS-approved scripts**, and where your carrier's e-app or process supplies verbatim text (TPMO disclaimer, enrollee authorization), read the carrier's text instead. Bracketed items are fill-ins. The Spanish version mirrors the English and borrows Molina's phrasing for the SOA statement; have a carrier's Spanish disclaimer on hand.

**English (approx. 90 seconds of required speech, excluding the needs review, presentation and application fields)**

> **[Recorder already running]** "This call is being recorded for quality and compliance purposes. Is that okay with you? [Get 'yes'; if no, end the call.] My name is Isabel Fuentes, I'm a licensed insurance agent, and I am not with Medicare or the federal government.
>
> **[SOA]** Before we talk about any plans, I need your permission on what we'll cover. Today, [date], I'd like to discuss [Medicare Advantage plans / Part D prescription drug plans / Medicare Supplement — name only what you will discuss]. Agreeing to this does not obligate you to enroll in anything, will not affect your current or future Medicare coverage, and will not automatically enroll you in any plan. Do you agree to discuss those products today? [Get 'yes'.] Please confirm your full name and phone number for me.
>
> **[TPMO disclaimer — before any benefit is mentioned]** We do not offer every plan available in your area. Currently we represent [X] organizations which offer [Y] products in your area. Please contact Medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE to get information on all of your options.
>
> **[Needs review]** Tell me your doctors, your prescriptions and doses, your pharmacy, and what you're paying now.
>
> **[Presentation covering the PECL topics, then]** The Summary of Benefits and the plan's Star Ratings document are available at [carrier website] or I can mail them to you.
>
> **[Application fields, then read-back]** Let me read back what I have: [name, DOB, address, Medicare number, Part A/B dates, plan, election period, effective date]. Is all of that correct?
>
> **[Attestation — use the carrier's authorization text if provided]** You are now completing an enrollment request in [plan name] with an effective date of [date], under the [AEP/IEP/SEP] election period. [Read the enrollee authorization.] Do you agree and authorize this enrollment, or would you like to decline? [Get 'I agree.'] Please state your full name and date of birth. Your confirmation number is [number]; you'll receive your ID card and welcome packet from [carrier]."

## Conclusion

The October 1, 2026 rule changes turn phone enrollment from a two-touch process (SOA, wait 48 hours, call back) into a single continuous recording, and that single recording is now the SOA record, the sales-call record and the enrollment form at once. That is why the one habit that matters more than any script line is to start recording before the first word and to file the audio the same day for ten years; nearly every compliance failure a solo broker can suffer (no SOA proof, missing disclaimer, lost attestation) is a recording problem in disguise. For Isabel, the shortest honest path is to choose one or two "Model A" carriers whose e-app captures the voice signature on her own recorded line, run the ninety-second required script above in English or Spanish, and let the e-app's embedded authorization text and confirmation number do the rest.

Two items deserve verification before AEP opens on October 15, because the research could reach them only through snippets: the exact post-SHIP TPMO disclaimer sentence your FMO expects, and whether Humana and UHC currently permit independent field agents to voice-sign phone enrollments (the evidence leans yes, the only explicit "no" statements are pre-2022). Everything else in this report is corroborated across CMS, carrier and FMO sources, and none of it requires a step beyond recording, SOA, disclaimer, PECL, application, attestation and storage.
