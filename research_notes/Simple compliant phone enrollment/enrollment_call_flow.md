## Key Question 1: What is the shortest compliant sales-to-enrollment phone flow brokers use (required disclosures at top of call, TPMO disclaimer text as of 2026, PECL / Star Ratings)?

### Takeaway
The minimum compliant flow working brokers and carrier scripts converge on is: (1) recording notice + consent (before anything else, so consent itself is on the recording), (2) TPMO disclaimer delivered verbally *before any benefits are discussed* (CY2027 rule, effective Oct 1, 2026; SHIP reference removed), (3) SOA confirmed/already on file, (4) needs review (doctors, drugs, pharmacy, budget), (5) plan presentation incl. PECL topics, (6) tell beneficiary where to access the Star Ratings document and Summary of Benefits, (7) application data captured in the carrier e-app, (8) enrollment attestation / voice signature (or three-way call to carrier line), (9) confirmation number and "what happens next." Forum posters openly admit some agents skip disclosures, but every FMO/carrier source treats the disclosures and a full presentation as mandatory.

**TPMO disclaimer (text and timing as of Oct 2026)**

- The CY2027 final rule was published in the Federal Register April 6, 2026 (doc 2026-06600, 91 FR 17384) — [Federal Register CY2027 final rule (PRIMARY; could not be fetched)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/06/2026-06600/medicare-program-contract-year-2027-and-certain-contract-year-2026-policy-and-technical-changes-to)
- Pre-change (through Sept 30, 2026) wording per CFR as summarized: "We do not offer every plan available in your area. **Pre-Enrollment Checklist (PECL) and Star Ratings on telephonic enrollments**
- 42 CFR 422.2267(e)(4): "The PECL is a standardized communications material that plans must provide to prospective enrollees with the enrollment form... For telephonic enrollments, the contents of the PECL must be reviewed with the prospective enrollee prior to the completion of the enrollment." (search excerpt) — [42 CFR 422.2267 (PRIMARY)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.2267); eCFR mirror: [eCFR 422.2267](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-V/section-422.2267)
- This telephonic PECL review was added by the CY2024 final rule: "we proposed to update §§ 422.2267(e)(4) and 423.2267(e)(4) to require that plans review the PECL with the prospective enrollee during telephonic enrollments" — [CY2024 final rule, Federal Register (PRIMARY)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/04/12/2023-07115/medicare-program-contract-year-2024-policy-and-technical-changes-to-the-medicare-advantage-program)
- Star Ratings: 42 CFR 423.2267(e)(17) (Part D) states "For telephonic enrollment, the beneficiary must be verbally told where they can access the Star Ratings Document." The CMS enrollment guidance restates that for telephonic enrollment the beneficiary must be told verbally where the Summary of Benefits and Star Ratings Document can be accessed (search excerpt) — [42 CFR 423.2267 (PRIMARY)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/423.2267); [CMS MA Enrollment & Disenrollment Guidance (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy-2024-ma-enrollment-and-disenrollment-guidance.pdf)
- PECL covers eight topics: eligibility, providers, drug costs, health needs/costs, premiums, plan benefits, effect of coverage change, administrative items; "must be reviewed during the call, before submission, and a verbal waiver doesn't excuse it" — [Open Exam Prep AHIP study guide (SECONDARY, unverified)](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ahip-medicare/enrollment-disclosure-requirements/pre-enrollment-checklist)
- A secondary source claims plans decide whether the PECL is read verbatim or reviewed conversationally — [IntuitionLabs TPMO article (SECONDARY)](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/cms-tpmo-definition-regulations)
- SCAN (carrier rule): "Plan Star Rating document must always be presented" to the beneficiary during electronic enrollment and is listed among paper-enrollment materials (search excerpt) — [SCAN Agent Portal enrollment page (PRIMARY, carrier)](https://agentportal.scanhealthplan.com/enrollment)

### Inferences
- Shortest defensible flow (synthesized from the carrier scripts and the CFR): Recording notice/consent -> TPMO disclaimer (pre-benefits) -> confirm SOA on file -> needs review (doctors/drugs/pharmacy/budget) -> present plan (covering the 8 PECL topics) -> state where SoB and Star Ratings can be accessed -> collect application fields -> read enrollee authorization/attestation and get verbal "yes" -> give confirmation/tracking number and next steps. Everything from the recording notice forward should be on one continuous recording.
- Because the Oct 1, 2026 rule moves the TPMO disclaimer to "before benefits are discussed," brokers can legally complete recording consent and SOA before the disclaimer; but most FMO playbooks still place it in the first 60 seconds for simplicity.
- Carrier scripts (Humana Multiplan, Independent Health) are templates agencies redline; the CFR does not mandate one verbatim CMS script for agent-led e-app enrollments (CMS approval of scripts applies to the plan's telephonic enrollment mechanism).

## Key Question 2: Which carriers accept a broker-recorded voice signature vs. require a call to their line (Humana, UHC, Aetna, Anthem, Wellcare, SCAN, Alignment, Devoted)? How do brokers take the application by phone?

### Takeaway
Carriers split into three models: (A) agent-recorded voice signature captured inside the carrier e-app or on the agent's own recorded line (UHC LEAN/JarvisEnroll Voice Signature, Wellcare/Aetna via Ascend "RATE," Devoted on a recorded line, Independent Health script); (B) carrier/IVR recorded line that issues a code the agent keys in (Humana IVR T-Sig three-way call, Anthem voice-signature recording tool, SCAN and Clover three-way confirmation lines); (C) carrier callback to the beneficiary for the signature (Devoted when agent is on an unrecorded line). Several sources (dated) say Humana and UHC historically restricted phone *enrollment* by field/independent agents to authorized call centers; current 2026 policy could not be confirmed.

### Inferences
- Model A (agent-recorded voice signature inside e-app): UHC (LEAN/JarvisEnroll Voice Signature), Wellcare and Aetna (Ascend RATE), Devoted (if agent is on a recorded line), Independent Health. Model B (carrier/IVR line issues a code): Humana IVR T-Sig three-way, Anthem recording tool, SCAN and Clover three-way confirmation. Model C (carrier callback): Devoted unrecorded-line path.
- Platforms built for a recorded agent line (RATE, JarvisEnroll voice) collapse the application and signature into one call; code-issuing lines add a three-way step and typically require inbound calls (Anthem) and the SOA to already be on file.
- Eligibility of *independent* (non-call-center) agents to use Humana IVR and UHC voice signature is the most uncertain point; the restrictive statements are all pre-2022 forum posts and may have been relaxed when CMS made recording universal in Oct 2022.

### Gaps
- No 2026 primary Humana or UHC document was retrievable stating whether independent agents may complete phone enrollments with voice signature; FMO sources imply yes (JarvisEnroll Voice Signature guide exists; Humana Enrollment Hub telephonic signature) but the current restriction status is unverified.
- Alignment Health: no telephonic enrollment process document found.
- Aetna 2026: current RATE/voice-signature instructions for MA not found; only 2015-2020 broker flyers.
- Field-by-field order of the Humana PY2026 Multiplan and Independent Health 2026 scripts could not be read.

## Key Question 3: What exactly must be on the enrollment recording / attestation under CMS (completing an enrollment request language, consent, confirmation number)?

### Takeaway
CMS enrollment guidance requires that a telephonic enrollment (a) follow the approved script, (b) include the individual's agreement to be recorded, (c) include a clear statement that the individual understands they are requesting/completing an enrollment, (d) capture all required enrollment elements, (e) include a verbal attestation of intent to enroll, and (f) provide a tracking/confirmation number; the recording itself serves as the enrollment form and is retained 10 years.

### Cited Findings
- CMS MA Enrollment and Disenrollment Guidance: "For requests accepted by approved telephonic enrollment mechanisms, the application date is the date of the call. The call must have followed the approved script, included a clear statement that the individual understands they are requesting enrollment, and have been recorded." (search excerpt) — [CMS MA Enrollment & Disenrollment Guidance (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy-2024-ma-enrollment-and-disenrollment-guidance.pdf); Part C&D consolidated version: [CY2025 C&D Enrollment Guidance (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy-2025-cd-enrollment-and-disenrollment-guidance.pdf-0)
- Same guidance: "For enrollment requests obtained during a face-to-face interview or telephone request, the determination of election period eligibility can be made at that time. For enrollment requests made using paper, or via an electronic enrollment mechanism or the Medicare OEC... the organization is not required to contact the applicant to confirm eligibility if the enrollment request includes the applicant's attestation of election period eligibility." (search excerpt) — [CMS enrollment guidance (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy-2024-ma-enrollment-and-disenrollment-guidance.pdf)
- CMS 2005 memo establishing telephonic enrollment: "Each telephonic enrollment request must be recorded and include a statement of the individual's agreement to be recorded, all required elements necessary to complete the enrollment, and a verbal attestation of the intent to enroll. Plans must develop scripts for this process, and those scripts must obtain CMS approval under the marketing guidelines before use." Phone enrollments were limited to inbound calls; caller had to be told they were completing an enrollment (search excerpt) — [CMS 10/5/2005 memo (PRIMARY, historical)](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/health-plans/healthplansgeninfo/downloads/ch2memomaosfinal10-05-05.pdf)
- CMS 2011 cost-plan memo: "The cost plan must record the audio of each telephonic enrollment request, including the attestation of intent to enroll. The telephonic enrollment mechanism must include a tracking mechanism, such as a confirmation number, so the individual has evidence that CMS received the request." (search excerpt) — [CMS Cost Plan Alternate Enrollment Mechanisms memo 8/8/2011 (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/Eligibility-and-Enrollment/MedicareMangCareEligEnrol/downloads/CostPlanAlternateEnrollmentMechanismsMemo882011.pdf); same elements repeated in [MMP Enrollment Guidance 2013 (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/mmp-enrollment-manual-6-14-13-508-clearpdf)
- CMS explains "when an enrollment occurs over the telephone, the enrollment portion of the call must be recorded because the recording can serve as the enrollment form and proof that the beneficiary attested to an intent to enroll" (search excerpt attributed to CMS via secondary) — [Informed Plus Choice: 6 vs 10 years (SECONDARY)](https://informedpluschoice.com/blog/medicare-call-recording-retention-10-years/); record-retention basis cited as 42 CFR 422.504(e)(1)(iv)/423.505(e)(1)(iv) — [Klariqo (SECONDARY)](https://klariqo.com/blog/medicare-tpmo-call-recording-rules-2026/)
- CMS FAQ (Oct 2022): CMS "cannot recommend a particular brand or mechanism" for recording — [CMS Agent/Broker Marketing FAQs 10-19-2022 (PRIMARY)](https://CMS.gov/files/document/agent-broker-marketing-faqs-10-19-2022.pdf)
- Carrier attestation examples: Independent Health: "This call is being recorded and that will serve as your electronic signature." and "(20) Enrollee Authorization... I understand that my signature..." — [Independent Health scripts (PRIMARY)](https://www.independenthealth.com/content/dam/independenthealth/broker/broker-partner-resources/documents/2026-telephonic-enrollment-script.pdf); Humana DMS-044: enrollee says "yes," states name and DOB; must be offered the option to say "no" — [Humana DMS-044 (PRIMARY)](https://humana.kmsihosting.com/dotnet/application/downloadcontent2.aspx?content_id=28498); PFS Med Supp voice-recording script: "Without a complete voice signature, a phone application cannot be submitted to the plan for enrollment." — [PFS Insurance Med Supp Voice Recording Script (SECONDARY)](https://pfsinsurance.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Medicare-Supplement-Voice-Recording-SCRIPT-INTERNAL_BROKERS-1.pdf); PFS telephonic enrollment training script — [PFS (SECONDARY)](https://pfsinsurance.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Final-Telephonic-enrollment-training-Script.pdf)
- "When does a call become an enrollment recording": once the beneficiary is told they are completing an enrollment request, agrees to recording, gives the required information, and verbally confirms intent to enroll; before that point the call is the marketing and sales portion (search excerpt) — [Informed Plus Choice (SECONDARY)](https://informedpluschoice.com/blog/when-does-a-medicare-call-become-an-enrollment-recording/)
- UPMC guidance (via CareValue): verbal-attestation method requires a CMS-approved script read word for word; electronic enrollment does not require a CMS script — [CareValue: UPMC telephonic guidelines (SECONDARY)](https://www.carevalue.com/blog/upmc-telephonic-calls-and-enrollment-guidelines)
- 2026 compliance scripts site: "All requirements must be met and recorded for telephonic enrollments to be valid." — [Insurance Space Scripts 2026 (SECONDARY, vendor)](https://www.insurancespacescripts.com/)

### Inferences
- Minimum enrollment-recording content: (1) consent to record; (2) identity (name, DOB, Medicare number) and all application elements; (3) explicit statement "you are completing an enrollment request in [plan]" and election period attestation; (4) PECL review and where to find Star Ratings/SoB; (5) enrollee authorization read and verbal "yes/I agree" with name and date (and a real opportunity to decline); (6) confirmation/tracking number given.
- The e-app platforms (JarvisEnroll, Ascend RATE, Enrollment Hub) embed the carrier-approved attestation text, so brokers using them satisfy the "approved script" element without a separate CMS-filed script.

### Gaps
- Exact current Chapter 2 section numbering and verbatim telephonic script requirements could not be read (CMS PDF fetch blocked).
- No current CMS text found that requires the confirmation number to be given to the beneficiary for *agent-assisted e-app* enrollments (the confirmation-number requirement is documented for plan telephonic mechanisms).

## Key Question 4: Whole-call recording: required or not, and retention period now (6 vs 10 years after CY2027 final rule)?

### Takeaway
Recording in their entirety is required for all marketing, sales, and enrollment calls with beneficiaries (incl. the audio of web-based calls); non-marketing/service calls are not required. The CY2027 final rule (published Apr 6, 2026; marketing provisions effective Oct 1, 2026) shortened TPMO marketing/sales recording retention from 10 to 6 years (audio for first 3 years, audio or complete transcript for years 4-6), while the enrollment recording remains an enrollment record subject to 10-year retention.

### Cited Findings
- 42 CFR 422.2274(g)(2)(ii) (as codified post-CY2027, per Cornell LII excerpt): TPMOs must record and keep marketing and sales calls for at least six years; audio required for the first three years; years four through six may be audio or "a complete and accurate transcript" (search excerpt) — [42 CFR 422.2274 (PRIMARY)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/422.2274); eCFR: [eCFR 422.2274](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-V/section-422.2274)
- CMS summary: finalized "with modification, to shorten the required retention period for marketing and sales call recordings by MAOs and Part D sponsors from 10 years to 6 years"; "Plans must retain audio recordings for the first 3 years, after which they may keep either audio recordings or complete transcripts for the remaining 3 years" — [HealthEdge CY2027 summary (SECONDARY)](https://healthedge.com/resources/blog/cy-2027-medicare-advantage-final-rule-rate-announcement-5-updates-payers-need-to-know); [Applied Policy (SECONDARY)](https://www.appliedpolicy.com/cms-finalizes-cy-2027-changes-to-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-to-implement-part-d-redesign-update-star-ratings-and-reduce-administrative-burden/)
- Scope wording in CMS Agent/Broker Training & Testing Guidelines: TPMOs must "record all marketing, sales, and enrollment calls, including the audio portion of calls via web-based technology, in their entirety" — [CMS CY2027 Agent & Broker Training & Testing Guidelines (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy2027-agent-broker-training-testing-guidelines-pdf.pdf); [CY2026 version (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy2026agentbrokertrainingtestingguidelinespdf.pdf); [CY2025 version (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy2025-agent-broker-training-testing-guidelines-revised.pdf)
- CMS FAQ Oct 2022: requirement applies to all TPMOs incl. independent agents/brokers and captive agents; "A plan must ensure that all calls between a TPMO and a beneficiary are recorded"; applies to inbound and outbound; effective Oct 1, 2022 for Jan 1, 2023 effective dates — [CMS Agent/Broker Marketing FAQs 10-19-2022 (PRIMARY)](https://CMS.gov/files/document/agent-broker-marketing-faqs-10-19-2022.pdf); HHS mirror — [HHS guidance (PRIMARY)](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/agent-broker-marketing-faq)
- CY2024 final rule narrowed scope: "call recording is limited to marketing, sales, and enrollment calls"; "calls other than those involving marketing, sales, and enrollments do not have to be recorded" — [LegalClarity CMS call recording (SECONDARY)](https://legalclarity.org/cms-call-recording-requirements-rules-and-retention/); [PSM: understanding the new CMS call recording changes (SECONDARY)](https://www.psmbrokerage.com/blog/psm-guide-understanding-the-new-cms-call-recording-changes)
- Ritter: any MA/MAPD/PDP enrollment meetings not physically in person must be recorded; recordings should run "in their entirety – including the enrollment" — [Ritter FAQs (SECONDARY)](https://ritterim.com/blog/faqs-about-the-medicare-call-recording-requirements/); [Ritter Medicareful FAQs (SECONDARY)](https://medicareful.ritterim.com/blog/faqs-about-the-medicare-call-recording-requirements)
- EnrollHere: "you must record all calls that could lead to a client enrolling in an MAPD, MA, or PDP plan"; requirements "are active year-round" — [EnrollHere call recording script (SECONDARY)](https://enrollhere.com/blogs/cms-call-recording-requirements)
- Recording must start before the consent step so consent is captured; record holds, warm transfers, conference/three-way calls and transfers to enrollment teams in their entirety; no signed waiver allows skipping recording; if beneficiary refuses, end call and offer in-person — [NIMBroker (SECONDARY)](https://nimbroker.com/2025/07/09/call-recording-medicare/); [Agility/Enroll Insurance (SECONDARY)](https://blog.enrollinsurance.com/2024/08/are-you-recording-your-medicare-advantage-interactions/); [NCC Agent CMS call recording communication (SECONDARY, FMO PDF)](https://nccagent.com/app/uploads/GS-NATIONAL-CMS-CALL-RECORDING-COMMUNICATION-FINAL.pdf); [New Horizons: how to record & store calls (SECONDARY)](https://blog.newhorizonsmktg.com/how-to-record-and-store-calls-for-aep-cms-compliance-changes)
- Enrollment recordings stay at 10 years: "Telephone enrollment portions remain on the separate 10-year retention track, while marketing and sales recordings move to a six-year minimum on October 1, 2026" — [Informed Plus Choice: 10 years now, 6 years Oct 1 (SECONDARY)](https://informedpluschoice.com/insurance-agents/medicare-call-recording-requirements/); [Informed Plus Choice CY2027 recording (SECONDARY)](https://informedpluschoice.com/blog/medicare-call-recording-requirements-agents-cy2027/); [Tech Savvy Insurance (SECONDARY)](https://techsavvyinsurance.com/blog/medicare-call-recording-retention-2027/)
- Conflicting claim: Klariqo argues the enrollment portion must be kept "eleven" years (10 years from end of contract year) — [Klariqo (SECONDARY, unverified)](https://klariqo.com/blog/medicare-tpmo-call-recording-rules-2026/)
- Effective date for new marketing/communications policies: "applicable for all contract year 2027 marketing and communications, beginning October 1, 2026"; rule published April 6, 2026 — [Tech Savvy Insurance (SECONDARY)](https://techsavvyinsurance.com/blog/medicare-call-recording-retention-2027/); CMS CY2027 changes doc — [CMS (PRIMARY)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/changes-medicare-advantage-organization-medicare-drug-plan-sponsors-marketing-guidelines.pdf)
- Scope conflict among secondary sources: one says all marketing/sales calls incl. web-audio must be recorded in entirety; another advises confirming whether the requirement now attaches only to calls where enrollment is discussed — [TMS Brokerage (SECONDARY)](https://tmsbrokerage.com/2026/09/18/the-cy2027-medicare-marketing-rules-go-live-october-1-heres-the-final-prep-playbook-for-independent-agents/); [Skycom call center guide (SECONDARY)](https://www.skycomcallcenter.com/blog/healthcare/medicare-marketing-guidelines/)
- Carrier practice: Golden Care guide says "recorded calls are stored for 10 years to be compliant with CMS requirements" (Nov 2025, pre-rule) — [GoldenCare Voice Signature Guide (SECONDARY)](https://www.goldencareagent.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/VoiceSignatureGuide.pdf)
- S.5149 (117th Congress) proposed exempting independent agents from 422.2274(g)(2)(ii); no evidence it was enacted — [Congress.gov S.5149 (PRIMARY)](https://www.congress.gov/117/bills/s5149/BILLS-117s5149is.htm)

### Inferences
- Practical broker rule: record the whole sales call from before the consent line through the confirmation number; this single recording satisfies both the TPMO marketing/sales recording (now 6 years) and the enrollment-record requirement (10 years) if the file is retained for 10 years. Brokers who split marketing vs enrollment retention gain little; most FMOs advise keeping everything 10 years.
- Service/non-marketing calls (claims help, ID cards) do not require recording under the post-CY2024 scope.

### Gaps
- The verbatim codified text of §422.2274(g)(2)(ii) after the CY2027 amendment was only seen as a search excerpt; the Federal Register and eCFR pages were unreachable.
- The exact operative date of the 6-year retention change (June 1, 2026 general effective date vs Oct 1, 2026 CY2027 marketing applicability) is reported inconsistently by secondary sources.
- The 10-year enrollment-record retention citation (422.504(e)(1)(iv)) was reported only by secondary sources and could not be confirmed against the CFR.
