## Key Question 1: Can the SOA be taken at the start of the same call now? (Status of the 48-hour rule, CMS-4208-F3, June 1 vs Oct 1, 2026, and the May 2026 HPMS SOA FAQ memo)

### Takeaway
Yes. The CY2027 final rule (CMS-4208-F3, published April 6, 2026, 91 FR 17384, FR doc. 2026-06600) deleted the 48-hour waiting period from 42 CFR 422.2264(c)(3) and 423.2264(c)(3). The rule's general effective date is June 1, 2026, but CMS's own HPMS "Scope of Appointment FAQs" memo (dated May 25/26, 2026) states the new SOA rules apply to CY2027 marketing beginning **October 1, 2026**. As of today (Oct 10, 2026) an agent may obtain the SOA and proceed directly to a personal marketing appointment on the same call. The SOA itself is still required for every personal marketing appointment, including inbound calls.

### Cited Findings
- The CMS HPMS FAQ memo (hosted copy) is dated May 26, 2026, addressed to MAOs, PDP sponsors and 1876 Cost Plans, and says (snippet): "on April 6, 2026, as a part of the Contract Year (CY) 2027 final rule, CMS finalized new rules related to the Scope of Appointment" at "42 CFR §§ 422.2264(c)(3) and 423.2264(c)(3)" and "eliminated the 48-hour waiting period required between the SOA completion and a personal marketing appointment." — [CMS HPMS Scope of Appointment FAQs (Hall Render hosted copy, PDF)](https://webdocs.hallrender.com/wp-content/uploads/HPMS-Scope-of-Appointment-FAQs.pdf) (primary; fetched only as snippet)
- Same memo (snippet): the new rules "apply to CY 2027 marketing beginning October 1, 2026" and the memo "says the new rules are applicable for all CY 2027 marketing and communications, beginning October 1, 2026." — [CMS HPMS SOA FAQs](https://webdocs.hallrender.com/wp-content/uploads/HPMS-Scope-of-Appointment-FAQs.pdf) (primary, snippet)

### Inferences
- The "June 1 vs Oct 1" confusion is a difference between the rule's *effective date* (June 1, 2026, the date the CFR text changed) and CMS's stated *applicability* to CY2027 marketing (Oct 1, 2026). CMS's own FAQ memo and slide deck both say Oct 1, and FMOs told agents to keep following 48 hours through Sept 30, 2026. Since today is Oct 10, 2026, the distinction is moot: same-call SOA is permitted under either reading.
- Carrier/FMO materials lag CMS; some 2026 carrier handbooks still state the 48-hour rule. Agents should expect carrier compliance departments to update guidance for AEP 2027 but to possibly retain stricter internal rules (Quotedeck notes carriers/FMOs "may keep stricter internal rules").

### Gaps
- Could not read the Federal Register preamble or the full FAQ memo text; the exact memo date (May 25 vs May 26, 2026) is unresolved between Hall Render and other sources.
- Could not confirm from the eCFR full text whether 422.2264(c)(3) now contains any residual timing language (e.g., "prior to the appointment") beyond "agree upon and record the scope ... before the appointment."

## Key Question 2: Which SOA elements are CMS-mandated vs optional (FMO/carrier add-ons)?

### Takeaway
CMS does not publish a model SOA form or verbal script. The element list that carriers/FMOs treat as "CMS-required" is consistent across Aetna, BCBS, Humana, PSM and others: (1) product types to be discussed, (2) date of appointment, (3) beneficiary contact info, (4) agent contact info, and (5) a statement that there is no obligation to enroll, current/future enrollment will not be affected, and automatic enrollment will not occur. Elements such as "I may be compensated," listing every product type, the TPMO disclaimer, and recording-consent language are *separate* CMS requirements or carrier/FMO add-ons, not part of the SOA itself.

### Cited Findings
- Aetna producer FAQ (Sept 2023) and BCBS Illinois/Oklahoma producer FAQs (Oct 2023), both citing 42 CFR §§ 422.2264(c)(3) and 423.2264(c)(3): "the SOA form must include the product types to be discussed, date of appointment, as well as beneficiary and agent contact information. It must state that there is no obligation to enroll, current or future Medicare enrollment status will not be impacted, and automatic enrollment will not occur." — [Aetna via PFS Insurance](https://pfsinsurance.com/news/medicare/aetna-091423); [BCBSIL](https://www.bcbsilcommunications.com/newsletters/nftb/2023/1011/stories/NLT_PROD_NFTB_IL_101123_SCOPE_OF_APPOINTMENT.html); [BCBSOK](https://www.bcbsokcommunications.com/newsletters/nftb/2023/1011/stories/NLT_PROD_NFTB_OK_101123_SCOPE_OF_APPOINTMENT.html) (carrier, secondary)
- Hall Render: "CMS does not provide a sample SOA form; however, CMS does provide guidance on what must be included in a valid SOA." — [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2026/06/01/cms-revises-medicare-advantage-marketing-guidance-for-scope-of-appointment-forms/) (secondary)
- Regulatory text (eCFR snippet) requires only that the plan/agent "agree upon and record the scope" before the appointment, "in writing" for in-person; the eCFR excerpt did not list fields. The field list appears to come from CMS sub-regulatory guidance (historic MCMG / HPMS Q&A), not the CFR. — [eCFR 422.2264](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-V/section-422.2264) (primary, snippet)
- PSM Brokerage Voice SOA page: "The recording should identify the beneficiary, the agent, the date, the product categories authorized for discussion, and confirm that there is no obligation to enroll." Also: the Voice SOA "doesn't replace recording the full sales call where that's required" and agents should "follow any carrier-specific Voice SOA script." Lists product categories "Medicare Advantage plans, Medicare Advantage Prescription Drug plans, Stand-alone Part D prescription drug plans, Medicare Supplement plans." — [PSM Brokerage](https://www.psmbrokerage.com/blog/voice-soa-scope-of-appointment) (FMO, secondary)
- PSM on required scripted language generally: "the required language can include a recording disclosure, a TPMO disclaimer, scope of appointment language, and carrier-required closing statements, and ... when a carrier requires exact language, it must be used exactly." — [PSM Brokerage call recording guide](https://www.psmbrokerage.com/blog/psm-guide-understanding-the-new-cms-call-recording-changes) (secondary)
- EnrollHere vendor guide (snippet) asserts the no-obligation language "must appear on the SOA form itself—it's not sufficient to simply state it verbally during the call"; this conflicts with CMS's May 2026 memo accepting an audio recording as the SOA record for non-in-person appointments. — [EnrollHere Academy](https://academy.enrollhere.com/Scope-of-Appointment-SOA-Compliance-1afbe273c76380b58a58d5d0107b1825) (secondary; contradicted by [CMS memo](https://webdocs.hallrender.com/wp-content/uploads/HPMS-Scope-of-Appointment-FAQs.pdf))
- Brokers' Broker compliance toolkit lists "a recorded verbal SOA via a compliant recorded phone process" as a valid method and warns never to rely on "a verbal, undocumented 'yes.'" — [Brokers' Broker](https://www.brokersbroker.com/compliance-toolkit/) (secondary)
- Compensation disclosure: no source found requiring a spoken "I may be compensated" statement as part of the SOA. 42 CFR 422.2274 governs compensation structures and requires disclosure of referral fees paid, not a spoken script line. — [eCFR 422.2274](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-V/section-422.2274) (primary, snippet); MedPAC: agents "must agree to the scope of appointment via a document or phone call prior to meeting with potential enrollees." — [MedPAC](https://www.medpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Medicare-agents-MedPAC-03.25sec.pdf)
- Medigap.com (consumer/agent reference): "CMS can approve a script read verbatim on a recorded line, and the beneficiary must state that they agree to talk about these products"; agreeing to MA is not agreement to discuss PDP or Medigap. — [Medigap.com](https://www.medigap.com/faqs/medicare-scope-of-appointment/) (secondary)
- Sources conflict on whether Medicare Supplement needs an SOA: Medigap.com says yes; another says Medigap is outside the CMS call-recording rule — [HealthReformQuotes](https://individuals.healthreformquotes.com/sign-medicare/mapd/enrollment-dates/regulations/soa-scope-of-appointment/) (secondary). (Regulatory note: Medigap is state-regulated and outside 42 CFR 422/423; carriers often include it on SOA forms anyway.)
- Call-recording retention changed in CY2027 rule: sources conflict — "6 years, audio for years 1–3 and audio or transcript for years 4–6" ([Informed Plus Choice](https://informedpluschoice.com/blog/medicare-call-recording-requirements-agents-cy2027/)) vs "3 years, down from 10" ([Medicare Rights Center](https://www.medicarerights.org/medicare-watch/2026/04/16/final-2027-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-rule-increases-plan-pay-and-relaxes-marketing-restrictions)). Older vendor pages still say 10 years ([AgentTech](https://agenttech.io/blog/telephonic-scope-of-appointment)).

### Inferences
- **CMS-mandated (per carrier readings of CMS guidance):** product type(s) agreed to; date; beneficiary ID/contact; agent ID/contact; no-obligation / no-effect-on-enrollment / no-automatic-enrollment statement; beneficiary's affirmative agreement; a retrievable record (recording or electronic record for phone).
- **Separate CMS requirements that often get bundled into the "SOA script" but are not SOA elements:** TPMO disclaimer (now "before benefits discussed," not first 60 sec); recording of the sales/enrollment call under 422.2274(g)(2)(ii); agent identifying as a licensed sales agent not from Medicare.
- **Carrier/FMO add-ons (optional per CMS):** reading out every product category as a menu; "I may be compensated"; "Do you consent to being recorded?"; POA/authorized-representative questions; carrier-specific closing statements; the FMO preference to try text/email SOA before voice.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the current CMS sub-regulatory text (MCMG successor / HPMS Q&A) that originally listed the SOA fields; the field list is attested only via carrier FAQs citing CMS.
- No source resolves whether CMS requires the beneficiary's *consent* to the SOA recording as an SOA element (see Q4).

## Key Question 3: What is the shortest verbal SOA brokers actually use that compliance accepts? (Verbatim scripts found)

### Takeaway
In practice, independent brokers either (a) read the carrier/FMO's telephonic script (Integrity "MULTIPLAN_IMG_AGTSCRIPT26_M" for Integrity-affiliated agencies; Molina, MetroPlus, Presbyterian, UHC scripts for carrier-specific flows), or (b) read their own 3–4 sentence script built from the CMS elements on a recorded line. The shortest form seen across carrier scripts is a single disclosure sentence (no obligation / no effect / no auto-enrollment) + a list of product types + "Do you agree?" + name/phone/date confirmation. Full verbatim scripts could not be fetched; verbatim fragments are below.

### Inferences
- **Distilled minimum verbal SOA (synthesized from the CMS element list and the shortest carrier wording above; not itself a CMS-approved script):**
  > "This call is recorded. I'm [Name], a licensed insurance agent, not with Medicare. Before we discuss any plans, I need your permission on what we'll cover. Today I'd like to discuss [Medicare Advantage / Part D prescription drug plans / Medicare Supplement — name only what will be discussed]. Agreeing to this does not obligate you to enroll, won't affect your current or future Medicare coverage, and won't automatically enroll you in any plan. Do you agree to discuss those products today, [date]? ... Please confirm your name and phone number."
  Elements: recording notice, agent ID, product types, no-obligation/no-effect/no-auto-enroll, affirmative agreement, date, beneficiary ID, agent ID (implied by recording metadata; many carriers want agent name/NPN stated). Approximately 60–75 seconds.
- Compliance departments at carriers (Molina, UHC, Presbyterian) accept only *their* verbatim scripts for telephonic enrollment; independent brokers using a generic recorded SOA are accepted at the FMO level (PSM, InsuranceSpaceScripts, Brokers' Broker) provided the CMS elements are present and the recording is retained.
- The TPMO disclaimer is now required "before benefits are discussed," so it can be read right after the SOA rather than in the first minute; some scripts still front-load it.

## Key Question 4: Must the beneficiary consent to recording (state two-party consent, e.g., California)?

### Takeaway
CMS requires that sales/enrollment calls be recorded (42 CFR 422.2274(g)(2)(ii)) but no source showed CMS requiring the beneficiary's *consent* to the recording. Consent is a state-law matter: California Penal Code § 632 requires all-party consent and California applies it when any party is in California; an announcement at the outset with the caller continuing is treated as consent. Carrier scripts (Presbyterian) nonetheless ask an explicit "Do you agree to this call being recorded?" and vendors recommend affirmative consent in every state.

### Cited Findings
- CMS recording mandate: "42 CFR 422.2274(g)(2)(ii) and 423.2274(g)(2)(ii) require recording Medicare marketing, sales, and enrollment calls, including audio from web-based technology." None of the sources say CMS requires consumer consent. — [Informed Plus Choice state recording laws](https://informedpluschoice.com/insurance-agents/state-call-recording-laws/) (secondary)
- California Penal Code 632 makes it illegal "to intentionally record or eavesdrop on any confidential communication without the consent of all parties involved." — [Shouse Law](https://www.shouselaw.com/ca/defense/penal-code/632/) (secondary, law firm)
- "California courts and the California Attorney General have consistently applied Penal Code 632 to calls where even one party sits in California at the time of the call." — [LeadCompliant](https://leadcompliant.com/articles/state-laws/california-attorney-general-recording-phone-calls-consent-law) (secondary)
- Kearney v. Salomon Smith Barney: "disclosure at the outset of the call can avoid violating section 632." — [Omni Law](https://omnilawpc.com/california/recording-conversations-california-law/) (secondary)
- Consent "can be as simple as announcing the recording and continuing after everyone agrees." — [RecordingLaw.com](https://www.recordinglaw.com/party-two-party-consent-states/california-recording-laws/audio/) (secondary)
- Vendor guidance: "in two-party states you need affirmative verbal consent"; recommends asking "Do you consent to being recorded?" in every state and warns against passive wording like "By staying on the line, you agree to be recorded." — [AgentTech CMS-compliant enrollment calls](https://agenttech.io/blog/cms-compliant-enrollment-calls); [EnrollHere call recording laws](https://enrollhere.com/blogs/call-recording-laws-insurance) (secondary)
- Sources disagree on the list of all-party states (twelve commonly cited; one vendor also treats Michigan, Montana, Nevada conservatively). — [EnrollHere](https://enrollhere.com/blogs/insurance-call-recording-compliance-checklist) (secondary)
- Carrier practice: Presbyterian script requires a verbal yes/no to "Do you agree to this call being recorded for compliance purposes?" — [PHS script](https://onbaseext.phs.org/PEL/DisplayDocument?ContentID=OB_000000043596); Ritter/Integrity: inbound callers "hear a recording notice for quality assurance and training." — [Ritter](https://ritterim.com/blog/faqs-about-the-medicare-call-recording-requirements/)
- Molina (Spanish guide): agent must "avisar al inicio que la llamada se graba para garantizar la calidad." — [Molina 2023 Spanish inbound guide (PDF)](https://molinaagentcenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/2023-Medicare-Telephonic-Enrollment-Call-Guide-Inbound-SPANISH.pdf)

### Inferences
- Minimum safe practice: open every call with a recording notice ("This call is being recorded") before any substantive content; for California (and other all-party states) obtain an explicit "yes," since the CMS-mandated recording cannot be turned off and a refusal means the sales conversation cannot proceed.

### Gaps
- No primary CMS text found addressing recording consent; the Kearney holding was not verified against the opinion.

## Key Question 5: Any Spanish verbal SOA scripts in use?

### Takeaway
Yes, carrier-level Spanish telephonic scripts exist (Molina 2023 inbound/outbound Spanish call guides) and many carriers publish Spanish SOA forms ("Alcance de la cita"), but no FMO/independent-broker Spanish verbal SOA script surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- Molina 2023 Medicare Telephonic Enrollment Call Guide, Inbound, SPANISH (Y0050_23_750_LRInbndEnrllSCR_M): agent announces recording at the start; beneficiary "no está obligado a dar información de salud" except for eligibility; "no hay obligación de inscribirse en los planes, y la llamada no cambia la inscripción actual o futura ni inscribe automáticamente en un plan"; agent asks the beneficiary to confirm understanding, verifies name and phone, and describes the agreed meeting. Document is 2023 with mixed Spanish/English. — [Molina Spanish inbound guide (PDF)](https://molinaagentcenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/2023-Medicare-Telephonic-Enrollment-Call-Guide-Inbound-SPANISH.pdf); outbound Spanish: [Molina Spanish outbound guide (PDF)](https://molinaagentcenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/2023-Medicare-Telephonic-Enrollment-Call-Guide-Outbound-SPANISH.pdf) (carrier)
- Spanish SOA *forms* (written) from Imperial Health Plan (Oct 2025; scope must be documented before any sales meeting "cara a cara o telefónica"), Baylor Scott & White (beneficiary "has no obligation to enroll ... the agent will not automatically enroll"), MetroPlus, ConnectiCare, Kaiser, HealthSun. — [Imperial SOA SP (PDF)](https://imperialhealthplan.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/SOA_C-SP_F.pdf); [BSW SOA SP (PDF)](https://www.bswhealthplan.com/SiteCollectionDocuments/Individual/Medicare/Y0058_Scope-of-Appointment-SP.pdf); [MetroPlus SOA SP (PDF)](https://metroplus.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/MPH_MCR_Scope_of_Appointment_Conf_Form_2024_Spanish_FILLABLE_508C.pdf); [HealthSun SOA SP (PDF)](https://healthsun.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Scope-of-Appointment-Spanish.pdf) (carrier)
- Medicare.gov Spanish consumer publication on meeting with agents (11593-S) exists. — [Medicare.gov 11593-S (PDF)](https://es.medicare.gov/publications/11593-s.pdf) (primary, consumer)

### Inferences
- A Spanish verbal SOA can be built by translating the CMS elements, using carrier form language as the model, e.g. (synthesized, not a CMS-approved script): "Esta llamada está siendo grabada. Soy [nombre], agente de seguros con licencia; no trabajo para Medicare. Antes de hablar de planes, necesito su permiso sobre los productos a tratar hoy: [Medicare Advantage / planes de medicamentos Parte D / suplementos de Medicare]. Aceptar no le obliga a inscribirse, no afecta su cobertura actual o futura de Medicare y no lo inscribe automáticamente en ningún plan. ¿Está de acuerdo en hablar de esos productos hoy, [fecha]? Confirme su nombre y teléfono, por favor."

### Gaps
- No FMO (Integrity/Ritter, SMS, PSM, NCC, AGA) Spanish verbal SOA script found; no 2026 Spanish telephonic guide found (newest is Molina 2023).
